! Description: DNS level blocklist for video entertainment: streaming services, live
!              sports, creator and short video, torrent indexers, trackers, cloud
!              torrent and debrid services. Audio only music services are left alone
!              on purpose, YouTube Music included. Written in AdGuard DNS filtering
!              syntax, the same syntax the AdGuard DNS filter uses.
! Homepage: https://github.com/aman-xurrent/video-dns-blocklist

!          v2fly/domain-list-community.

! Total rules: 760 blocking rules, 35 exception rules
!
! ============================================================================
! WHAT THIS BLOCKS
! ============================================================================
!
! The line is drawn at video, not at entertainment in general. Anything you watch
! is blocked. Anything you only listen to is not.
!
! BLOCKED. Video streaming: YouTube, Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+ and JioHotstar,
! Max, Hulu, Paramount+, Peacock, Apple TV+, Crunchyroll, SonyLIV, Zee5, MX Player,
! Tubi, Pluto and the rest. Live and creator video: Twitch, Kick, Rumble, TikTok,
! Dailymotion, Vimeo, Bilibili. The piracy side: torrent index sites, public
! tracker announce hosts, cloud torrent and debrid services such as webtor.io,
! Seedr, Bitport, ZbigZ, put.io, Offcloud, Premiumize, Real-Debrid, AllDebrid and
! TorBox, plus Stremio, unlicensed movie and sports streaming, and game repacks.
! Also MEGA and Plex, blocked by explicit request rather than by that rule.
!
! NOT BLOCKED. Every music service. Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music, YouTube
! Music, SoundCloud, Deezer, Tidal, Pandora, JioSaavn, Gaana, Wynk, Bandcamp,
! Last.fm, Qobuz, Anghami and the others are all reachable. They are named one by
! one in the allowlist at the bottom of the file so the intent is explicit.
!
! ============================================================================
! HOW THE SYNTAX WORKS
! ============================================================================
!
! ||example.com^     blocks example.com and every subdomain under it.
! @@||sub.example.com^   is an exception. It puts one name back.
!
! In AdGuard, an exception rule beats a normal blocking rule no matter where the
! two rules sit in the file. That is what lets ||youtube.com^ and
! @@||music.youtube.com^ live in the same list.
!
! Where to load it:
!   AdGuard Home    Filters > DNS blocklists > Add blocklist (both rule types work)
!   AdGuard DNS     Custom filtering rules or a custom blocklist URL
!   Pi-hole         Adlists understand ||domain^ fine, but they do NOT reliably
!                   understand @@ exceptions. On Pi-hole, add music.youtube.com to
!                   the Domains > Allow list by hand after adding this list.
!   NextDNS         Denylist import, then allow music.youtube.com by hand.
!
! ============================================================================
! THE YOUTUBE MUSIC PROBLEM, STATED HONESTLY
! ============================================================================
!
! YouTube and YouTube Music are not separate services underneath. They share the
! same backend. These four are used by both and are therefore NOT blocked anywhere
! in this file, on purpose:
!
!   googlevideo.com          the actual audio and video streams
!   (youtubei.googleapis.com used to be here. Since v1.3.0 it is BLOCKED on
!    purpose, which is what kills both native apps. See the strict tier below.)
!   ytimg.com and ggpht.com  thumbnails and album art
!   googleusercontent.com    channel and artist images
!
! Since v1.3.0 youtubei.googleapis.com is blocked, so the native YouTube app and
! the native YouTube Music app are both dead: they talk to InnerTube and nothing
! else. YouTube on the web is blocked too, on every device on your network, along
! with every youtube.com link anyone sends you. YouTube Music in a browser is the
! only thing that survives.
!
! DNS filtering cannot do better than that. Splitting YouTube video from YouTube
! Music at the DNS layer is impossible while they share a backend hostname. If you
! need the apps gone too, remove them from the device or use an MDM app block.
!
! Verified on 2026-08-28 by pulling the real music.youtube.com HTML and reading it:
!
!   Works with youtube.com blocked. The player JavaScript is served from
!   /s/player/ on music.youtube.com itself, not from www.youtube.com. The
!   InnerTube API call is same origin. Audio comes from googlevideo.com and album
!   art from yt3.googleusercontent.com, neither of which this file blocks. So
!   playback, search and your library all work.
!
!   BREAKS with youtube.com blocked. Signing in. The sign-in link on
!   music.youtube.com hands Google a continue URL of
!   https://www.youtube.com/signin?action_handle_signin=true..., so the browser
!   is sent to www.youtube.com after you authenticate, and that hostname is
!   blocked by this file. If you are already signed in, nothing happens. If you
!   need to sign in fresh, do one of these:
!     - sign in on the YouTube Music phone app, which uses accounts.google.com
!       and youtubei.googleapis.com and never touches www.youtube.com, or
!     - pause the filter, sign in once in the browser, then turn it back on, or
!     - temporarily add @@||www.youtube.com^ to your rules, sign in, remove it.
!
!   Cosmetic only. A few onboarding shelf images are served from
!   www.youtube.com/img/music/tastebuilder/, so those tiles show as broken
!   images. Nothing else is affected.
!
! ============================================================================
! STRICT TIER, ON BY DEFAULT SINCE v1.3.0
! ============================================================================
!
! ||youtubei.googleapis.com^ is now an active rule, not a suggestion. That single
! line kills BOTH native apps, YouTube and YouTube Music, because both talk to
! the private InnerTube API and nothing else can reach it.
!
! What still works: YouTube Music in a browser at music.youtube.com. Verified in
! a live session rather than assumed. Every InnerTube call the web player makes
! goes same origin to music.youtube.com/youtubei/v1/ (player, next, browse,
! guide, search suggestions), and audio still streams from googlevideo.com. It
! never touches youtubei.googleapis.com.
!
! So the shape of this file is now: no YouTube video anywhere, no YouTube app,
! no YouTube Music app, and YouTube Music works in a browser tab.
!
! To go back to the softer behaviour where the native apps keep working, delete
! the ||youtubei.googleapis.com^ rule and add @@||youtubei.googleapis.com^ to the
! allowlist at the bottom.
!
! Do NOT block these. They break YouTube Music on the web too:
!
!   googlevideo.com   the audio stream itself
!   ytimg.com         thumbnails
!   ggpht.com         artist and album art
!
! Why there is no finer knob than this. Measured on 2026-08-28 by reading the
! live player response for three tracks:
!
!   track                         audio host              video host
!   Never Gonna Give You Up       rr5---sn-ci5gup-cagee   rr5---sn-ci5gup-cagee
!   Together Forever (remaster)   rr3---sn-ci5gup-cagee   rr3---sn-ci5gup-cagee
!   Together Forever (art track)  rr8---sn-ci5gup-cagr    rr8---sn-ci5gup-cagr
!
! Audio and video for the same track come from the identical hostname every
! time. The only difference is the itag and mime query parameters, and DNS never
! sees a query string. The host is also allocated per request, which is why the
! replica number moves between rr3, rr5 and rr8 inside one session. There is no
! stable, content typed hostname to write a rule against, so "block the video but
! keep the audio" is not achievable at the DNS layer. Even a YouTube Music art
! track carries video formats, because an art track is a still image encoded as
! video with the audio muxed alongside.
!
! ============================================================================
! HOW THIS LIST WAS BUILT
! ============================================================================
!
! Every domain in this file was resolved against 1.1.1.1 and 8.8.8.8 on
! 2026-08-28. Anything that answered NXDOMAIN was dropped, and anything that
! resolved to a known domain-parking IP was dropped. Domains with no A record at
! the apex but a live DNS zone were kept, because that is normal for CDN parents
! like nflxso.net, hdslb.com and aiv-cdn.net where all the traffic is on subdomains.
!
! Deliberately NOT blocked, to avoid collateral damage:
!   amazon.com, apple.com, google.com, googleapis.com, amazonaws.com
!   archive.org, torrent.ubuntu.com, torrent.fedoraproject.org (legal torrents)
!   jellyfin and emby (self hosted media servers)
!   academictorrents.com, legittorrents.info, linuxtracker.org (lawful torrents)
!   themoviedb.org, thetvdb.com, musicbrainz.org, trakt.tv, imdb.com (metadata
!     APIs that Jellyfin, Kodi, Radarr and Plex call; blocking them silently
!     breaks media libraries)
!   Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive (general cloud storage)
!   every music service, see the allowlist at the bottom
!
! Checked on 2026-08-28, because unblocking the music services while blocking the
! video ones only works if they do not share hostnames:
!   Amazon Music is the one that looks risky, because Amazon runs both services.
!   It is not, and the reason is in the names: aiv is Amazon Instant Video, pv is
!   Prime Video, atv-ps is the Amazon TV Playback Service. Every Amazon hostname
!   this file blocks is video specific, none of it is shared Amazon plumbing.
!   Corroborating check: music.amazon.in was fetched on 2026-08-28 and mentions
!   none of them, loading only music.amazon.*, m.media-amazon.com and CloudFront.
!   Be honest about the limit of that check though. The page is an 8 KB bootstrap
!   shell, so it is not the app's full endpoint list. The naming argument is what
!   carries this, not the grep. If Amazon Music ever does break, capture your DNS
!   query log while it fails and allowlist whatever Amazon hostname shows up.
!   Apple Music is music.apple.com and mzstatic.com. Apple TV+ is tv.apple.com.
!   Different hostnames, no overlap.
!   Spotify shares nothing with anything blocked here.
!   YouTube Music is the one real conflict, and it is handled by the exception
!   rule at the bottom. See the section above for exactly where it leaks.
!
! Expected collateral, not a bug: youtube.com/redirect is the hop every link in a
! YouTube video description goes through, and it is part of youtube.com, so those
! links die too. Same for youtu.be short links anyone sends you.
!
! WHAT CHANGED IN v1.2.0, AND WHY IT MATTERS
!
! v1.0 and v1.1 carried an extra 1,148 domains imported from The Block List
! Project. Every one had been DNS resolved, which proved they were alive. It did
! not prove they were piracy sites, and that turned out to matter. Sitting in that
! block were nasa.gov, worldwind.arc.nasa.gov, gutenberg.org, webcast.berkeley.edu,
! pbs.org and bittorrent.com. All six are legitimate. Each is on a torrent
! blocklist because it once ran a BitTorrent tracker to hand out something lawful:
! NASA satellite imagery, Project Gutenberg ebooks, Berkeley lecture recordings.
!
! The other 1,142 could not be checked by hand with any confidence, so the whole
! block was dropped rather than shipped on trust. Six known false positives in a
! spot check is enough to distrust the source. Coverage of the genuinely piracy
! shaped domains now comes from the regular expressions in blocklist.txt,
! which match on brand and shape instead of on an imported list nobody verified.
!
! Piracy mirror domains change every few weeks. The torrent and pirate streaming
! sections will need a refresh every month or two to stay useful. The licensed
! streaming section is stable and will not.
!
